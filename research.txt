AC basket research: full summary

All figures use prices to 9 November 2026. This is research, not investment advice.

1. Goal
Build a moderate-risk basket of Indian stocks across the air-conditioning value chain.
Pick stocks with 13 fixed rules, then test whether those rules would have worked in the past.
2. How companies were found
Starting list: 28 AC-linked companies from the top 1,000 NSE stocks, sorted by stage: raw materials, parts and contract makers, brands, commercial cooling, retail.
Backtest list: wider. I scanned the business descriptions of all 2,599 NSE companies for AC keywords.
Added 5 smaller names: VOEPL, Stallion Fluorochemicals, Triton Valves, Cubex Tubings, Onida.
Added 1 failed name: Fedders Electric (the old Lloyd Electric).
Excluded as false keyword matches: air coolers, industrial air compressors, engine radiators, process heat exchangers, HVAC maintenance services and similar.
3. The 13 decision metrics

A red counts 2 points and an amber counts 1. The first five (marked ★) are critical: a single red on any of them means Remove.

Metric	Green	Red
★ Daily traded value	≥ ₹10 cr	< ₹2 cr
★ Promoter pledge	≤ 10%	> 25%
★ Debt / equity	≤ 0.5	> 1.0
★ Interest cover	≥ 5×	< 2.5×
★ Loss years (last 5)	0	2 or more
Volatility (1 year)	≤ 35%	> 50%
Beta (5 years)	≤ 1.2	> 1.5
Worst fall (5 years)	≥ −45%	below −60%
Cash conversion (5 years)	≥ 0.8×	< 0.5×
ROE (5-year average)	≥ 15%	< 10%
Margin swing	≤ 3 pts	> 6 pts
P/E ÷ its 5-year median	≤ 1.3×	> 1.7×
PEG	≤ 2	> 3.5
4. How a stock is marked
Remove if:
AC is less than 20% of its business, or
it has any critical red, or
it has more than 8 points.
Watch if:
it has 4–8 points, or
its price history is under 3 years, or
3 or more of its numbers are missing.
Pass if it has 3 points or fewer and none of the above applies.
5. What we left out, and why

Shown in the sheet for context, but not used to decide:

Correlation with the Nifty and with the other AC stocks
Sales, profit and EPS growth
Stock returns (1, 3, 5 and 10 years)
Promoter holding and forward P/E
Exports: only KRN discloses it (16.6%)

Left out of the backtests only:

Promoter pledge: no history is available.
“Use the more cautious of two sources”: only one source has 10 years of statements.

Still to decide:

Debt to equity and cash conversion before about FY2016 don’t exist in machine-readable form. This blocks a 15-year test with identical rules.

Number checks

14 key numbers per company were checked against a second source: 341 matched, 31 differed, 20 had one source only.
Where the sources disagreed, the decision used the more cautious figure.

The pattern behind it

Most removed winners failed on low trading or deep past falls, meaning small, illiquid companies. They tend to surge in small-cap rallies (2020–2023).
In bad years the screen protected you. In June 2018 and 2019, removed stocks averaged about −30%, and Fedders Electric was wiped out (−84%, then −100%).
10. Key takeaways
The screen beats the Nifty consistently, by about 7 points a year.
It also misses the sector’s biggest winners, because those are small and risky when they start.
Pass + Watch did best: 27.6% a year, with the smallest worst fall (−21%).
Watch stocks beat Pass stocks in 7 of 9 years, but individual Watch stocks can still fall hard, for example Whirlpool −43% in 2025–26.
The evidence is limited: 8.4 years and 3–19 stocks a year is a small sample.
